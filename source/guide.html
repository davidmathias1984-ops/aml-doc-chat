Connells AML/CTF Quick Reference Guide

UPDATED 29/06/2024
- Change to TNHG instruction process (see Corporate section)
- Change to Court of Protection
- Requirement to provide proof of ownership on all Supportal tickets when unable to obtain the title
- Minor updates throughout.

The purpose of this page is to provide a quick reference guide to the AML/CTF Handbook.

The guidance on this page is simplified and is not designed to replace the detailed guidance in the AML/CTF Handbook.

IMPORTANT NOTE!

  • You must not set ‘For Sale’ / commence any marketing (including booking viewings, emailing SPL, call outs from branch etc.) on an instruction, or issue MOS on a purchase until you have carried out sufficient CDD or received FCP approval (as per the transaction type detailed within this QRG).
  • If FCP approval is required (in line with the guidance below) you MUST WAIT until you have received the FCP approval email before proceeding.
    Ensure you save a copy of this approval to Matchmaker (Appraisal tab > Documents > Other CDD Vendor/Purchaser > ‘FCP Approval Email Vendor/Purchaser’)

    Please ensure to expand all comments on the Supportal ticket before printing to PDF. The full thread must be visible for CMT review.     .

    Land Registry

    Legislation states we must take steps to establish all the legal owners of all properties we market. This is required for all transactions.

    Establishing Ownership

    You must ensure to establish the ownership with your vendor at the outset of the transaction with effective questioning establishing the clients understanding of the transaction type. Circumstances may have changed since they first purchased i.e., marriage/deaths/change of names etc. so ensure detail is captured.

    Help with Land Registry Queries

    The Land Registry Guidance Procedure can be found here

    If branch have a Land Registry query, branch must: follow all instructions in the Land Registry Guidance procedure, and:.

    • If the query is relating to a Land Registry issue only, please raise this on Supportal under transaction type ‘Land Registry ONLY’. Ensure to complete the form and all questions in full so FCP can assist correctly. If you have note been able to obtain the title you must answer 'No' to this question on the form.
    • Where branch are dealing with a transaction type that requires referral to FCP (i.e. Corporate, Trust, Deceased Estate etc.) as well as a Land Registry query on the same file then branch must raise the request under the relevant primary transaction type (e.g. Deceased Estate) and answer the Land Registry related queries within the form. Please DO NOT raise 2 separate tickets for each element, FCP may ask you to resubmit correctly which could cause delays.
    • Await confirmation from FCP before proceeding.

    Some Frequently Asked Questions on Land Registry

    Q: If a property is unregistered, do I refer to FCP and what can I obtain as proof of ownership?

    A: Double check the address being sold with your vendor to ensure the property is not registered under any other house name/number. If no titles available, refer to the FCP Team to confirm the property is definitely unregistered. If unregistered, the owner could provide one of the following to evidence ownership:

    • Mortgage statement (latest statement issued within last 12 months)
    • Title Deeds / Title Absolute
    • Written confirmation of ownership sent direct to branch from a regulated solicitor
    • Buildings Insurance or Buildings & Contents - latest policy document (Contents only is not acceptable) - if dealing with a deceased owner, this policy document must name the deceased
    • Signed transfer deed (TR1/TP1) sent directly to branch from a regulated solicitor
    • Confirmation of mortgage settlement.

      Once the property is confirmed as unregistered, FCP do not need to review the proof of ownership documents obtained from the above list unless you have a query / concern, or where this forms part of another transaction type requiring FCP referral (e.g.Corporate, Power of Attorney etc.) - in this case, proof of ownership must be obtained at the outset and provided for FCP to review.

      Do ensure to check with the vendor if the property is registered at Land Registry at the outset – this will ensure proof of ownership can be requested upfront where likely to be required and ensure you can handle the transaction more quickly.

      Q: My vendor has recently bought the property and Land Registry hasn’t been updated to show their name yet – how do I proceed?

      A:Branch still need to refer to the FCP Team. Branch must obtain confirmation of completion – this should include the name(s) of the new proprietors (owners), case reference for Land Registry application and the date that completion took place.
      We can accept this either as:

      - Email correspondence from the solicitor/conveyancer who lodged the application with Land Registry – this must be received directly from the conveyancer’s official email domain not forwarded to branch from the customer) and include all information requested above.

      OR

      - Letter from the solicitor/conveyancer (on letter headed paper) and with all requested information included.

      A TR1 form is not sufficient to evidence ownership unless sent directly to branch by a regulated solicitor/conveyancer and it is signed in line with published Land Registry guidance.

      NOTE: If re-selling quickly after purchase, consider why this is – do you understand the reason for quick resale and does it make sense as a transaction? If it doesn’t make sense or you have any concerns, consider raising a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) via this Supportal link.

      Q: My vendor’s name on their ID is different to the name on the Land Registry title – what do I need to do?

      A: You must obtain evidence of the name change and hold this on file/upload to Matchmaker alongside the customer’s proof of ID and proof of address documents. Documents you can use as proof of name change include:

      • Marriage Certificate
      • Deed Poll document
      • Legal divorce documentation if both the former and current names are stated within

      If you can obtain evidence, you do not need to refer to FCP.
      If you are unable to obtain evidence of a name change please refer to the FCP Team for guidance. This should be raised using the Supportal Customer Due Diligence form - transaction type ‘Private Individuals’ then ‘Name Change / Discrepancy’.

      Q: I’m selling a Park Home – how do I evidence ownership?

      A: Individual Park / Mobile Homes are not registered on Land Registry. Refer to the Land Registry Guidance Procedure for acceptable proof of ownership documents and how to override the Land registry check on Matchmaker. There is no need to refer Park Home instructions to FCP unless struggling to evidence ownership.

      NOTE: Land registry compliance portal on Matchmaker - Ticking all the boxes to confirm CDD is complete includes any referral to FCP Team. You will need to have full CDD (and approval from FCP on file if required) before these boxes are ticked.

      Raise a Customer Due Diligence Supportal ticket (Financial Crime Prevention)

    Private Individual (Representatives, Care Home residents, Customer in Prison)

    You MUST:

    • Obtain acceptable proof of ID and address for all owners/purchasers of the property and any representative(s) in line with the Verification of Identity (VoI) Form (Connells Group) or Verification of Identity (VoI) Form (Gascgoine Halman)– see below for more information if dealing with a representative or a customer in a care home.
    • PEP/Sanction screening must be completed on all parties, including representatives (either via Credas or Call Validate).
    • A VOI is required for each individual.
    • Tick ‘Instruction’ to enable the ‘ Land Reg Check ’ button and run the Land Registry title check
      (please note the property will not be instructed at this stage – you will need to tick ‘Instruction’ again to complete the instruction process once CDD/FCP approval is in place)

     

    Representative

    Who would be classed as a representative?

    A representative is anyone you are dealing with on behalf of the vendor/purchaser in relation to the property and where more than just access is being discussed (for example, we will be discussing viewings/feedback/offers with them or they are involved in any decision making – If you are unsure always check with the appraiser).

    A representative could be a spouse/partner/parent/son/daughter/friend (this list is not exhaustive).

    Example: Mr Smith is the sole owner named on the Land Registry title- he is happy for his wife to be involved in the sale of his property and for her to speak to branch about offers / detail approval.
    Is his wife a representative? YES!

    Example: Ms Jones gives you her lodger’s number to arrange access for viewings – the lodger lives at the property, but all other aspects of the sale are dealt with directly with Ms Jones.
    Is the lodger a representative? NO - they are acting as a ‘key’ only.

    For your representative(s) you must have:

    • Completed Representative form or written authority from the owner/purchaser of the property giving you authority to deal with their representative.
      If the vendor is providing you with their own authority letter, the letter must:
      - Be signed and dated
      - Confirm the names of both parties (vendor and representative)
      - State the property address
      - Be in place before you deal with the representative
      - The letter should also confirm how the representative can act/what they can do – i.e. Can they accept offers? Agree price reductions? (Is it a blanket authority or are there any limits/restrictions?)
    • Proof of ID and address for that individual – documents must be in line with the VOI form.
    • PEP/Sanction screening must be completed on representatives (either via Credas or Call Validate).
    • VOI form completed for the individual.
    • You must also have CDD for all property owners as well as the personal representative.

    Refer to the Personal Representative Guidance here

    Dealing with a Property Finder?
    For an individual follow the standard process above (CDD for the individual and Letter of Authority).
    For a Company property finder: obtain the letter of authority and follow Company/Corporate process below – you must have approval from FCP for the company check before you proceed.

    NOTE: where an additional contact number/second party is noted on MatchMaker / the property file, file notes must be on MatchMaker to clarify their involvement with the transaction.
    The Compliance Monitoring Team expect to see either completed due diligence and authority letter for the representative, or file notes to explain the relationship/reason for branch holding their contact details (made before any contact with the additional party has taken place).

    NOTE:Where you are dealing with a Power of Attorney transaction, you must only deal with the Attorneys or Replacement Attorneys appointed by the donor and named in the POA document. You cannot deal with an individual ‘representing’ the Attorney.

    NOTE:Where dealing with a Deceased Estate, a representative may be involved but the legally appointed executor/administrator (named on the Will or Probate) must make any final decisions relating to the property (e.g. accepting an offer, marketing price).


     

    Care / Nursing Home residents

    If your customer is resident in a care / nursing home then confirmation from the home is sufficient in lieu of ID and POR documents. The electronic PEP/Sanctions check must still be run – this may ‘Refer’ but this is acceptable provided there are no other warnings. Confirmation should be provided by the care home manager / Deputy Manager or a member of the administration team, and sent via email from the care home’s official domain directly to branch (not forwarded via the attorneys). FCP don’t need to review this unless you have concerns.


     

    Vendor / Purchaser in Prison:

    If your customer is incarcerated you will need to refer to FCP for guidance before proceeding.

    In this situation, you will likely be dealing with a representative acting on their behalf. This may be under a formal Power of Attorney document (please refer to the Power of Attorney section of the QRG below).
    Where there is no official power of attorney in place: you must still obtain written confirmation of the representative’s authority to act – this should be an Authority Letter/Representative form signed by the prisoner and witnessed by the prison Governor or senior member of prison staff. The convicted owner should sign the Agency Agreement and this should be witnessed by the Governor.

    You are still required to carry out due diligence on the incarcerated customer.
    In some cases where a solicitor or barrister is involved they may be able to assist in providing certified ID documents. If no solicitor is involved it can be difficult (or impossible) to obtain List A ID documents (note: branch are not expected to attend the prison to meet the customer).
    Instead, you must obtain a letter from the prison Governor confirming:

  • - individual’s name
  • - their DOB
  • - location of prison they are residing in (e.g. HMP Lewes)
  • - date of incarceration

    • You must also run PEP & Sanction screening (via Call Validate or Credas standalone checks). The check should be run against the customer at either the property or the prison address. FCP also need to understand why the customer is in prison and their usual address/place of residence.
    FCP will carry out additional searches and advise if any further steps need to be taken (for example, if linked to a serious offence potentially, or the offence is linked to a proceeds of crime restriction) before branch can proceed.

    You must not commence any marketing (including calling out, booking viewings, ‘Coming Soon’ marketing etc.), set For Sale or issue MOS until you have had FCP approval.

    Refer to the FCP Team via Supportal for guidance - make sure you:

    • Have already followed the guidance in the User Guides / on the VOI form:
      Credas User Guide
      MatchMaker Compliance User Guide
      VOI Form (Connells Group)
      VOI Form (Gascoigne Halman)
    • Give full details of the situation (i.e. why you are struggling to complete CDD / obtain documents and attempts made to obtain them) – FCP need to fully understand the transaction before they can advise or take a risk based approach.
    • Send across all documents you have already managed to get (including Call Validate reports, ID etc.)

    If you have referred to FCP for guidance, do not commence any marketing (including calling out, booking viewings, ‘Coming Soon’ marketing etc.), set For Sale or issue MOS until you have had FCP approval.

    Supportal - Financial Crime Prevention

    Prime Properties

    What is a Prime Property? Any property that is being marketed or sale agreed at £1m or over (or £2m and over for branches within the M25 and Gascoigne Halman offices).

    Prime Properties require Enhanced Due Diligence checks and must be approved by FCP before you proceed.

    If a property is instructed and then also sold at or above the Prime limit then you will need two FCP approvals- one for the vendor (before marketing) and one for the purchaser (before issuing MOS).
    If a property was marketed as a prime however has sold beneath the Prime limit then branch are not required to submit the purchaser to FCP for approval.
    If the property was not being marketed as a prime and subsequently has an agreed sale price at or above the Prime limit, then branch must submit both Vendor and Purchaser to FCP for approval.

    You must send all of the below documents to Financial Crime Prevention via Supportal:

    • Credas reports (or proof of ID, proof of address + Call Validate reports) for all vendors/purchasers and any representative(s).
    • VOI form (completed in full) for each individual.
    • Land Registry title (instruction only).
    • Any other supporting documentation (i.e. Power of Attorney document, Will, Trust document)
    • Confirmation on source of funds and proof of funds (purchase only). ***See Source of Funds, Gifted Deposits and Proof of Funds section below for further guidance.
      • Where cash funds are coming from the sale of a property, please ensure to include a copy of the MOS (if issued) or if MOS not yet issued, a link to the property listing on Rightmove/agent’s portal etc. to confirm the marketing price.

    Failure to attach all requested documents will delay approval.

    Please ensure to make FCP aware of any changes during the course of the transaction (change in marketing price, change of name/address, source of funds etc.)

    You must not set ‘For Sale’/commence marketing in any way (including ‘Coming Soon’, booking viewings etc.) and must not issue the MOS without an FCP approval reference.

    Supportal - Financial Crime Prevention

    Companies / Corporate

    If you don’t know or are unsure what type of company you are dealing with, please check with your point of contact at the company.

    Overseas Companies

    Please note where you are dealing directly with a company based outside of the UK the Financial Crime Prevention team will ask for additional information in line with the below:

    The “Register of Overseas Entities” is in force in the UK and affects the sale of property owned directly by or purchases made by an overseas entity. Overseas companies are required to register on Companies House if they buy or sell land in the UK and the Financial Crime Prevention Team will need confirmation of the company's OEID number.

    IMPORTANT: If you are made aware that a purchase is being changed into a new/different company name at any point during the transaction then you must refer to FCP immediately for updated corporate checks to be completed. You must pause the transaction until FCP can issue you with updated approval.

     

    You MUST:

    • Instructions - enable the ‘Land Reg Check’ button on the MatchMaker ‘Appraisal’ tab and run the Land Registry title check
    • Complete Credas or get appropriately certified proof of ID for your point of contact at the company.
    • Purchases – establish source of all funds i.e. mortgage + cash from business activity (for full cash purchases – you must obtain proof of cash funds). ***See Source of Funds, Gifted Deposits and Proof of Funds section below for further guidance.
    • Raise a Supportal ticket and complete in full. Ensure all relevant documents are attached and robust information on source of funds is provided (purchase only): .

      You must attach:

      - ID /Credas
      - Land Registry report or proof of ownership if branch are unable to obtain the LR title (instruction only)
      - Proof of cash funds (cash purchases only)
      - Evidence of authority to act (e.g. email communication from the official company domain) if your contact is not named against the company on Companies House

    PEP/Sanction checks will be completed by FCP.

    To avoid unnecessary delays, please ensure to provide FCP with detailed confirmation on source of all cash funds (how the company accumulated the funds) on the Corporate Customer ID form or in the body of your referral email.

    You must not set ‘For Sale’/commence marketing in any way (including ‘Coming Soon’, booking viewings etc.) and must not issue the MOS without an FCP approval reference.

    • Obtain email correspondence (sent from the official solicitor’s firm domain) and keep on file.
    • Proof of ID/address is not required. A CallValidate is not required.

    TNHG (The New Homes Group) ONLY - Part Exchange / Assisted Move

    There is no need to refer TNHG part exchange or assisted moves instructions to FCP for approval. All due diligence (including Land Registry title check) will be completed by TNHG before the instruction is passed to branch. The TNHG instruction email will confirm this, and this email must be uploaded to Matchmaker.

    Branch may set for sale immediately with no need for FCP review. The Land Registry title check can be overridden (reason: ‘Other’ then add ‘TNHG’ instruction’ to the comments box).

    Non-TNHG - Assisted Move

    If the proprietor / owner name(s) shown on the Land Registry title match exactly to the Vendor name on the instruction paperwork - you can proceed and set the property For Sale immediately. You do not need to refer to Financial Crime Prevention.

    Where the proprietors DO NOT MATCH to the instruction paperwork (e.g. only one party named; different surname)

    • If you have contact with the owners - you must get documentation to show the reason for the difference in name(s) - for example; CDD for second owner, marriage certificate showing maiden name to married name, death certificate for one deceased owner. You may then proceed to set the property 'For Sale'. You do not need to refer to Financial Crime Prevention.
    • If you do not have contact with the owners, you must make your 3rd party instructing company aware of the discrepancy. You may then proceed and set ‘For Sale’ immediately but must refer to FCP within 24 hours and provide FCP with evidence that you have notified the company of the discrepancy.


      Where FCP referral is required, you MUST upload the following documents on the Supportal ticket:

      • Instruction paperwork
      • Copy of email correspondence showing it has been sent from the official entity-associated email domain(e.g. @movewithus.co.uk).
      • Land Registry title (if available to branch). If LR title is not available, ensure to fill in the relevant fields on the Third Party Supportal referral form

      Non-TNHG - Part Exchange

      You may proceed and set the property 'For Sale' immediately but must refer to FCP within 24 hours.

      You must send the following documents to FCP:

      • Instruction paperwork
      • Land Registry title (or proof of ownership unable to obtain the title)
      • Evidence that correspondence has come from the official email domain

      Supportal - Financial Crime Prevention


      NOTE: Some examples of documentation to evidence a difference in name between Land Registry title and the instruction paperwork could be - CDD for second owner, marriage certificate or Deed Poll, death certificate for a deceased owner etc.

    Repossession / Corporate Probate

    Where you have a Repossession or Corporate Probate instruction from AMG, you may proceed immediately (unless specifically contacted by Financial Crime Prevention) – you do not need to refer AMG Repossession or Corporate Probate cases to FCP.

    All other transactions from AMG (i.e. Assisted Voluntary sales)

    Where you are not dealing with anything other than a Repossession/Probate instruction from AMG, you may proceed to market immediately but must send across to FCP for review – please include Land Registry title and instruction paperwork.
     

    Where your instruction comes from within Group (including Group Corporate Services / Corporate Lettings Team for Connells or Countrywide), branch must still run a Land Registry title check, but provided the name matches to that on the instruction paperwork, may then proceed.

    If there is a discrepancy between the name on the LR title and instruction paperwork, you must refer back to your instructing party and make them aware. You must await further confirmation from your point of contact that all required CDD is complete before proceeding. Keep this email on file.

    Countrywide branches: If you are accepting documents certified by a Countrywide branded office please ensure these are certified correctly before proceeding.

     

    Where dealing with Spring you do not need to refer to Financial Crime Prevention (FCP) - checks have been completed centrally by FCP and you can proceed without referral.

     

    Instruction

    Where instruction comes from Wayhome you do not need to refer to Financial Crime Prevention (FCP) and can proceed.

    Purchase

    Purchases made under Wayhome’s ‘rent to buy’ scheme will be agreed in the name of a LLP (i.e. Wayhome LLP-123).

    Branch do not need to refer in for corporate checks but must carry out due diligence on the individuals (Credas + VOI form OR Proof of ID and address, Call Validate + VOI form). Branch may then proceed once CDD carried out.

     

    Instruction (including Land)

    You MUST:

    • Complete a Land Registry title check.
      (Struggling to complete the title check? Make sure you have followed guidance LR Guidance / TIG and loaded to MatchMaker correctly).
    • Complete a Credas check, or get appropriately certified proof of ID and address for all customers. You can accept a Credas report from IAS provided it meets the requirements in the Connells User Compliance Guide.
    • Run PEP/Sanction screening on all customers (either via Credas or Call Validate).
    • Check the transaction type- follow relevant guidance in QRG first (i.e. Deceased Estate process). Obtain copies of any relevant documents from IAS.
    • Obtain any necessary approval from Financial Crime Prevention (FCP) and save to the property file.
    • THEN follow the IAS/MMoA process – refer to the Transaction Instruction Guide (TIG) on guidance on how to instruct a property being sold via Modern Method of Auction

    Purchase

    You MUST:

    • Give the customers a copy of the AML Leaflet for Customers. Save a note onto the Matchmaker Notes tab confiming that the AML leaflet has been provided to the purchaser(s).
    • Complete a Credas check, or get appropriately certified proof of ID and address for all customers.
    • Run PEP/Sanction screening on all customers (either via Credas or Call Validate).
    • Check the transaction type - follow relevant guidance in the QRG first if applicable (i.e. Corporate, Power of Attorney process).

    Cash Funds - Source/Proof of Funds, Gifted Funds and Overseas

    Source & Proof of Funds

    SOURCE of funds:
    Establishing source of funds is obtaining confirmation from the client as to where the funds first came from (where they are originally sourced).
    The original source of any cash funds must be established on all purchases and details noted on the VOI Form.
    Be aware that just 'savings' (without context to how these have been built up) is not sufficient as an original source, and that a customer saying they have ‘cash in the bank’ does not automatically mean that this is cash from their personal savings/surplus wages. You must ensure you have asked the customer for specifics (see below) as to how they have come by the funds.

    Some common examples of where funds could originate from (list is not exhaustive):

  • Personal savings from income/salary
  • Investments/stocks/shares
  • Equity/proceeds from a previous sale (of a property, business, other - please specify)
  • Cash from a business
  • Inheritance
  • Gifted or loaned

    • You will also need to establish that the source and amount of cash funds being provided is reasonable and consistent with what you know about your customer. If a large amount of cash is being used - does this make sense with customer's profile, their job role etc. As part of your CDD you must be satisfied as to the source of these funds and their legitimacy.

    For example – having £120,000 cash sourced solely from personal savings may make sense if you know that a couple purchasing together are both in established and well paid careers, but less so if the sole purchaser is (for example) a recent graduate and only working part time.
    In this scenario, the source of the £120,000 cash in the customer’s bank account may well be legitimate but it is likely not from personal savings- it is much more likely to originate from another source (perhaps an inheritance or gifted by family (or could be coming from a less legitimate source).


    PROOF of funds:
    Obtaining proof of funds is confirming/evidencing the availability of the funds. It is best practice to obtain proof of funds in all cases – these should be retained on MatchMaker and any additional information regarding source of funds noted on the VOI form to evidence your due diligence.

    How do we evidence cash funds? See some examples of ‘proof of funds’ documents (not an exhaustive list) below:

    • Proceeds of Property Sale - MOS / completion paperwork/ evidence of their house being for sale (such as online listing/property details, confirming chain check with their selling agent with notes on file, confirmation from regulated solicitor).
    • Mortgage Finance - documentation of mortgage being processed, Decision in Principle or offer details in writing from the broker / financial services professional.
      NOTE: Be aware of Mortgage Certificates without Lender details (this suggests that they haven’t been credit checked). This should be followed up with an email to the broker.
    • Cash Savings- evidence could include bank statements showing available funds, written confirmation from a regulated solicitor/registered IFA, written details of a legacy/inheritance, investment portfolio statements etc.
      NOTE: Remember where source is confirmed as 'savings' more specific information is required - is this solely savings from income/current employment, gifted funds, investments, rental income etc? .

    Branch staff need to be comfortable with the original source of funds before proceeding and be able to supply this information to FCP where required. If any concerns over source and/or proof of funds provided - raise a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR).


    Gifted Funds

    You must establish the original source of any gifted funds – how did the person gifting the money accumulate the money (e.g. wages from employment, investment dividends, inheritance, proceeds of property sale?) You must clearly note this on the VOI Form along with confirmation on how much is being gifted (if multiple gifters, note how much is coming from each).

    Where more than £100,000 of funds are being gifted to the buyer you must:

    • Carry out customer due diligence (Credas or proof of ID, proof of address and Call Validate check) on the person(s) gifting the funds as well as on the purchaser(s)
    • Establish the original source of the gifted funds and clearly note on file
    • You should also obtain proof of funds (best practice in all cases).

    You do not need to refer this to Financial Crime Prevention unless you have any concerns, or unless this requires referral for any other reason covered in the QRG (i.e. funds coming from High Risk Jurisdiction) – see below.

    Vulnerable Customers

    If you have any concern or suspicion that a customer is being exploited / taken advantage of, or that someone is acting against their best interests then please raise a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) – (see below) or email GroupMLRO@connells.co.uk immediately.

    What is a Vulnerable Customer?

    A vulnerable customer is someone who, due to their personal circumstances, is especially susceptible to harm, exploitation or being financially disadvantaged; particularly when a firm is not acting with appropriate levels of care.

    It’s important to remember that anyone could be considered a Vulnerable Customer, and this could be for a number of different circumstances, for example:

    • Disabilities / Learning difficulties
    • Financial Difficulties
    • Language Barrier
    • Knowledge (for example, First Time Buyer or not sold a property before)
    • Going through a significant life event (for example Bereavement, divorce or the birth of a child)

    Even if the individual is not being exploited, we may need to make adjustment in how we provide them with information and documentation. For help and guidance please reach out to the Group MLRO email address (GroupMLRO@connells.co.uk)

    SARs (Suspicious Activity Reports)

    Here are some red flags to look out for:

    • Benefit Fraud
    • Bribery
    • Difficulty obtaining ID
    • Drug Trafficking
    • Forged documents

    Agents Notes

    Standard Notes

    Can be issued without EA Compliance referral.

    In the below circumstances, Branch must include the relevant Agents Note within the property listing and on the Memorandum of Sale, when ready:

     
    Where ownership of the property has been confirmed by FCP as correct, the following should be used:

    Agents Note: The Land Registry title has yet to be updated with the Vendor’s details. Please ask the branch for more details.

     
    Where FCP have confirmed the property is unregistered and have ownership has been established by other means, the following should be used:

    Agents Note: It is our understanding that the Property is not registered at the Land Registry which is the case with a significant proportion of land across England and Wales. Your conveyancer will take the necessary steps and advise you as to time-frames for registration.

    Side Note; If the property is being sold through I Am Sold, the branch must ensure that IAS are aware of this as the property may not be appropriate for their timescales.
     
    Where this has yet to be applied for, or applied but not received, the following must be used:

    Agents Note: The sale of this property is subject to receipt of Grant of Probate. Please obtain an update from the branch with regards to the potential timeframes involved.

    FCP guidance on the Deceased Estate aspect is still required before proceeding.

     
    When the vendor has died without a Will, the following must be used:

    Agents Note: The sale of this property will be subject to receipt of Letters of Administration from the Probate Office. We ask that interested parties seek guidance as to the potential timeframes involved for this purchase with their conveyancer.

    FCP guidance on the Deceased Estate aspect is still required before proceeding.

     
    When the Vendor is selling their Park Home, the following must be used:

    Agents Note: There are a number of obligations on both sellers and buyers when completing the process for purchasing a park home. Sites often have requirements specific to the purchase and to ‘the site’ in general, which could include paying the site owners commission. Intending purchasers should satisfy themselves about any such requirements including any specific restrictions on occupancy or residential use of the home.
    Purchasers are strongly advised to take advice from a solicitor. Guidance can be sought from Park homes - GOV.UK (www.gov.uk)


    Non-Standard

    In all other instances and where a disclosure of Material Information is required, the EA Compliance Team (Connells), will provide you with guidance and a suitable Agents Note. Material information that requires disclosure can be found in many different sources including the Sellers Enquiry Form completed by your vendor, the Land Registry Title or documents that you have gathered for AML purposes. You must check all these documents for any information that may require disclosure.

    Interested parties need to be made aware of something that could affect their decision to view/offer. Failing to disclose material information is a breach of consumer protection laws and could be costly if a complaint is raised and upheld because you knew something and didn’t disclose it.

    In cases where something has been made known to you in your professional capacity, please contact your compliance team (EA Compliance for Connells brands) for advice and guidance. The use of an Agents Note must be discussed with the vendor before marketing so they understand the legal obligation to make a disclosure.

    For any queries surrounding completion of Agency Agreements / Sellers Enquiry Forms, please contact the EA Compliance Team - EAComplaints@Connellsgroup.co.uk for further guidance.

    The following types of cases should be referred to EA Compliance for review:

    • AA/SEF enquiries
    • Separation/Court Orders
    • Potential disclosures on Death Certificates
    • A New Build built in the garden of the titled property
    • Where a property title is being split (garage/land separated from title)
    • Creation of leasehold titles (a Freehold property split into flats)
    • Overage or Uplift clauses
    • Possessory Title shown on LR Title
    • Leasehold title anomalies (less than 80 years remaining)
    • Structural/cladding issues made known
    • Shared Ownership sale
    • Neighbour Disputes
    • Vendors who have been convicted of serious crimes - these must be immediately referred to both FCP and the EA Compliance team
